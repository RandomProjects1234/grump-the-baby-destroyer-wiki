Mrs. Honeywell's Jobs

The full mechanics behind the daily list: how it's planned, every job template with its exact goal formula, and the reward pools. See The Day for the loop this fits into, and Mrs. Honeywell for the character.

How the list is planned

The host plans the day's list from a seeded RNG (seed:quests:night), so every player in co-op sees an identical list without it ever being invented client-side.

  • Day 1 is always the fixed three: Get the lights working, Lunch time, Round them up — deliberately teaching fuel, food, and toddlers before anything harder shows up.
  • Day 2 onward, jobs are drawn from a shared pool: fuel, repair, clean, eat, lunch, bandage, rescue, searchRoom, lights, visit, hide, drawing, hamster, holocard, plus bandage a second time, crayon twice (only while Grump hasn't turned), and fuel, eat, rescue each added an extra time — weighting the "big three" survival jobs (and "Patch someone up") above the flavour ones. The pool is shuffled, then picked through in order.
  • List length: 3 jobs, rising to 4 from night/day 6 onward.
  • A job that can't happen in this school is skipped, not left broken. Each candidate is resolved to a real room before being accepted: lunch needs a cafeteria or kitchen; bandage needs a nurse's office and at least one toddler actually hurt that day; searchRoom needs a room type with at least 3 searchable props; every room-specific job (searchRoom, lights, visit, drawing, hamster, crayon, holocard) needs at least one room of an eligible type to actually exist in this generated school. If a candidate resolves to nothing, the planner moves to the next pool entry instead of shipping a list one job short — a bug fixed in commit df20bce where quest lists could previously come up short.

Reward pools

On completion, a job's reward is drawn from its tier's pool using a per-job seeded RNG (seed:reward:night:index), then placed in the crib.

How progress is tracked

Each job on the list listens for exactly one event type (shown above). Progress only advances when that event fires and, for room-specific jobs, the event's room matches the job's assigned room. A job completes the instant its progress reaches its goal — there's no need to finish everything at once, and jobs can be worked in any order or in parallel.
